# 🥤 Clayton Christensen's Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)

Formulated by Professor Clayton Christensen (Harvard Business School), **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)** shifts focus from superficial user demographics (age, income) to the fundamental context and motivation driving consumer behavior.

---

## 💡 Core Premise

> *"Customers do not buy products or services; they 'hire' them to perform a specific job and achieve progress in a specific context."*

---

## 🧩 The Three Dimensions of a "Job"

Every "Job" consists of three interwoven dimensions:

1. **Functional Dimension**: The practical task or objective to be accomplished (e.g., getting a quick energy boost during a morning commute).
2. **Emotional Dimension**: How the user wants to feel while performing the job (e.g., feeling energized, avoiding guilt).
3. **Social Dimension**: How the user wants to be perceived by others (e.g., looking professional, being a responsible provider).

---

## 🥤 The Milkshake Example & Non-Obvious Competition

Christensen famously analyzed why a fast-food chain's morning milkshake sales surged:
- **Demographic Assumption**: Target commuters aged 25-45.
- **JTBD Discovery**: Customers "hired" a thick milkshake because it lasted 30 minutes during a long, boring morning drive, required only one hand to drink, and kept them full until lunch.
- **True Competitors**: Not other milkshakes, but **bagels** (messy), **donuts** (sticky/guilt-inducing), **bananas** (eaten too quickly), or **boredom**.

---

## 💡 Application in Project PRODIGY

In **PRODIGY Stage 1 & 2**, student leads use JTBD to identify non-obvious competitors (e.g., paper logs, WhatsApp groups) when deploying healthcare or agricultural tools for rural communities.
